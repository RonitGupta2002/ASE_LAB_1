What I think about AI in software engineering today

AI tools are excellent at the mechanical part of the job: boilerplate, test scaffolding, rewriting code between languages, explaining unfamiliar codebases. However, they are still much weaker at the parts that actually decide whether a system works such as requirements, architecture, etc.

Where software engineering will be in 5–10 years

Writing code manually will be of much less significance and major part of the job will be specifying intent and reviewing generated changes in the pull requests. The gap between engineers who have put in real effort in understanding the systems deeply and those who only prompt will widen to the point where the latter will almost hold no value.

My hot take on AI

AI could take over the world if the rate at which the progress is being currently made never plateaus.

What I think about AI in software engineering today

AI is a useful tool for speeding up repetitive tasks, generating boilerplate code and helping developers learn new concepts. But AI still makes mistakes and therefore understanding fundamentals ist still essential. Good developers use AI as a tool but not as a replacement for critical thinking.

Where software engineering will be in 5–10 years

Software engineers will mostly spend their time with designing the system, validating requirements and checking AI-generated solutions. Hence even small teams will be able to build software products that currently requires large engineering teams.

My hot take on AI

Developers who can only work with AI will struggle more than developers who barely use it.

What I think about AI in software engineering today

Agents have made it much faster to get from an idea to something that runs. The difficulty lies in still deciding what to build and verifying that it is correct. It will take a lot of time and effort to reach a fully solved trust yet.

Where software engineering will be in 5–10 years

In the next 5-10 years, there will be much less manual coding, regardless of language or domain. What will truly matter is the number of people who deeply understand code and can review it. Writing code will get cheaper, but knowing whether it is correct, secure and maintainable will stay valuable.

My hot take on AI

What I think about AI in software engineering today

I see AI as a fundamental shift in how we approach problem-solving and software development. However, I believe its progress should be accompanied by responsibility, transparency, and proper regulation. We should not focus only on what AI can achieve, but also consider its environmental footprint, particularly its growing demand for electricity and water.

Where software engineering will be in 5–10 years

I believe software engineering will gradually shift from writing code line by line to designing systems, defining problems, and making critical architectural decisions. AI will take over much of the repetitive implementation and testing, allowing engineers to focus on creativity, innovation, and complex problem-solving. The value of an engineer will increasingly depend on how well they can think, design, and guide AI rather than simply how fast they can code.

My hot take on AI

AI won't eliminate the need for software engineers; it will expose the difference between those who can think and those who can only code.
